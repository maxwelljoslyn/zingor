# Real Estate

The **Real Estate** page (linked in the header) records the land and buildings the party owns. There are two kinds:

- a **parcel** is a piece of land;
- a **building** is a structure, which may stand on a parcel or stand on its own (a town house on somebody else's street, say).

Deleting a parcel does not demolish the buildings on it; they stay recorded, no longer attached to any land.

## Ownership

A parcel/building is owned by **characters**, not by players. Ownership is a plain set: a mill can belong to one character, or to three of them jointly, and there are no shares or percentages. Owning through a character is what lets a player who runs two characters keep their holdings separate.

Whoever plays an owning character can edit it: its name and notes, which parcel a building stands on, and the owner list itself. Everyone else can look but not touch.

A character who dies or retires keeps their holdings. Marking a character inactive does not remove them from any owner list, so it stays editable by the rest of its owners; hand it over deliberately, by adding and removing owners, rather than leaving it to a default.

## Recording a Parcel/Building

At the bottom of the Real Estate page, name the parcel/building and choose which of your characters owns it. A building can be placed on an existing parcel at the same time, or attached later from its own page. You must have a character before you can record one, because the first owner has to be one of yours.

## The Parcel/Building Page

Click a parcel/building's name to open its page. It lists the owners (with the player behind each), the parcel a building stands on, and the buildings standing on a parcel.

Owners see an **Edit** form for the name and notes, an **Add Owner** control that offers every character in the campaign, a **Remove** button beside each owner, and a **Delete** button. A parcel/building must keep at least one owner, so the last owner's Remove button is not offered. You may remove your own last character from a parcel/building you co-own; that is how it changes hands, and the remaining owners can always add you back.
